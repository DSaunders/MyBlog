## Not enough whitespace

## Listing every job in detail
If you've had lots of job, no need to write half a page on each.
A paragraph on recent jobs, a short sentence on older but relevant jobs and a 1-liner for anything early you still 
wish to include (if you have 10 years experience, your internship/work experience doesn't need more than a single line 
unless it's relevant to the job you're applying for. Likewise if you've had a career change you might want to list old jobs 
to show you've been working etc. but no detail is needed)

## Telling me everything
Leave something to ask at the interview.
The role of the CV isn't to get the job, it's to get to interview. Tell me what I need to know to short-list you,
I don't need all the specific detail, we'll discuss that at interview.

## Not tailoring it to the job
You don't always need to do this, but I would suggest that you taylor it to the job - highlught the things that are mentioned in the job descripton/advert.

In particular, the intro/about me/{better name?} section can address the specific requirements in the job descripton.
This is a useful place to talk about what you are looking for, and to sell yourself.

I can guarantee that when we get a collection of CVs, atleast half will describe the person as a 'passionate developer who loves to solve problems for 
customers etc. etc.'.. I'm sure it's true, but in my opinion there is more you can do with this space.
(if you're sending a covering letter, you can use that for this purpose, and this section in the CV can be more generic.. but I'd still taylor it 
to the job a little bit)

## Too long
1-2 pages max in my opinion

## Listing every technology you've ever touched
Because you wrote a ruby script for your build server, it doesn't need to go on your CV.
I'm going to assume that you can pick up enough to get by if we have things like that, I want to know what you're comfortable with to work day-to-day.

I understand this is sometimes the CV equivalent of keyword stuffing to get through HR or automated CV systems.. I have my own feelings
 about them and my condolences if you have to do this.

## Being 'expert' at everything
I see a lot of CVs with a comptency level at the top, next to a list of technologies.
I suppose this is a good idea, but if you put everthing at 10/10 or 'Expert' then it either looks arrogant or will just be ignored.
I don't have this on my CV, I mention techinologies I work with day-to-day, and list each lanagauge briefly against all of the (relevant) jobs 
in my history
