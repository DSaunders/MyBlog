10 years ago I switched careers from a physical engineering job to being a programmer.

This is my advice to my past self, and other self-taught programmers just starting out.


# Ask questions
.. lots of questions.

Good peers will be happy to spend the time answering them, and will respect you more for asking.

Never. Stop. Asking. Questions.

# Look for interesting work
Optimise your career moves for doing interesting work with smart people.

If you're fortunate enough that you can afford it early in your career, choose jobs that challenge you over jobs that pay more. 

You will grow quicker and it will pay off later.

# Not all experience is equal
Experience isn't measured purely in years.

You can have 10 years experience, or experience the same year 10 times.
They are not the same thing.

Optimise for the former, keep doing new things..

# Read

Ask people you respect for recommendations, but you can't go wrong if you start with some classic books:

- Code Complete
- The Mythical Man-Month
- The Pragmatic Programmer


# Learn some fundamentals

Your mileage may vary, but learning data structures and how some common algorithms work has made me a better developer. 

If nothing else, it helps with the imposter syndrome.

# Be part of the community

Follow interesting people on Twitter. 

Read and comment on people's blogs.

Go to talks and conferences if you can. Seeing people talk about things they are passionate about has inspired me more than anything else has.

# Learn to ship real sofware

If you have the time and inclination outside of the day job, work on side projects.

Build real things that interest you. Learn to ship actual software, however small.

If you want to leave work at work though, that's cool too. No pressure (see 👇)

# Give yourself a break

You don't have to 'catch up' to people with CS degrees.
The gatekeepers are not worth your time.

Give yourself a break, you're doing great.
